What this means

A yield curve plots the yield on government borrowing against the time until repayment. A curve where longer terms pay more than shorter ones is called normal; where shorter terms pay more, inverted; where they pay about the same, flat. The spread is the long-term yield minus the short-term yield, in percentage points. Lessons on yield curves explain what economists read into each shape; this page shows the published numbers and makes no forecast.

With a short-term yield of 3.90 % and a long-term yield of 4.50 %, the spread is +0.60 points, so the curve is normal (longer terms pay more).

Show the maths
spread = y_long − y_short = 4.5000 − 3.9000 = 0.6000 points
normal:   spread > +0.25
flat:     −0.25 ≤ spread ≤ +0.25
inverted: spread < −0.25

Ipotesi e limiti

  • The numbers are the ones each publisher released, copied without change. A missing point means the publisher did not release a value for that maturity on that date.
  • Par yields and spot rates are different measures, so each series is drawn on its own axes. The comparison feature compares two dates of one series.
  • The labels normal, flat and inverted describe the spread with a fixed band: normal above +0.25 points, inverted below −0.25 points, flat in between. They describe the published numbers and are not a forecast.
  • The file holds about one year of history for each series.
