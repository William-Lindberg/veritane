Comparing and Negotiating Credit Offers

A repeatable method for lining up loan offers and understanding what lenders can move on.

Intermediate·5 min (Simple) · 8 min (Detailed)·Updated 6 November 2026

Read first: Il costo reale di un prestito

Key points

  • Offers line up fairly on APR, total cost, flexibility and penalties, not on the headline rate alone.
  • Checks before approval can be soft (not visible to other lenders) or hard (visible on your credit report).
  • Rates are often negotiable, especially when there is a competing offer.
  • Brokers may be paid by lenders; how they are paid is a question they answer.
  • Not taking any offer is a valid outcome.

Simple: plain words. Detailed: exact figures, codes and methods.

In short: two loans with different rates can cost the same, and two with the same rate can cost very different amounts. A simple grid of the same facts for every offer makes the differences visible.

Simple version

A comparison grid

Put each offer in its own column and fill in the same rows:

Offer A Offer B
Amount ¤10,000 ¤10,000
Interest rate 6.9% 6.5%
Upfront fee ¤0 ¤300
Term 3 years 3 years
Monthly payment ¤308.31 ¤306.49
Total paid, including fees ¤11,099.30 ¤11,333.64
APR (including the fee) 6.9% about 8.6%
Early repayment fee none 1 month’s interest

These are example numbers. Offer B has the lower rate but costs about ¤234 more in total, because of the ¤300 fee. The APRAnnual percentage rate (APR)The yearly cost of borrowing as a percentage, usually including interest and most fees, with exact rules that vary by country.Read more about Annual percentage rate (APR) (the yearly cost of borrowing including most fees) catches this: B’s APR is higher.

Other rows worth adding: whether the rate is fixed, whether payment breaks are possible, and whether insurance or other products are bundled in.

APR ComparisonTurn the rate, fees and term of two to four loan offers into one comparable yearly cost (APR) and a total cost in money.

Five questions

  1. What is the total amount I repay, in ¤, including every fee?
  2. Is the rate fixed, and if not, what does it follow?
  3. What does paying early cost?
  4. What happens if I miss a payment?
  5. Who is paid what for arranging this loan?

Representative APR

Adverts often show a “representative” or “typical” APR. In some countries this is the rate that a set share of successful applicants receive, not the rate everyone gets. Your own rate depends on your application. The rate in a personal quote is the one to put in the grid.

Soft and hard checks

Lenders look at your credit reportCredit reportThe file of a person's borrowing and repayment history held by a credit bureau or registry.Read more about Credit report before lending. A soft check (sometimes called a quotation search) shows an estimate and is not visible to other lenders. A hard check happens when you formally apply, and other lenders can see it. Several hard checks in a short time can lower a credit scoreCredit scoreA number summarising a credit report that lenders use to judge risk; the models and scales vary by country and lender.Read more about Credit score in some scoring systems. Many lenders and comparison services now offer soft-check quotes.

Brokers

A brokerBrokerAn intermediary who arranges a product or a trade for a client and is paid by fee, commission or both.Read more about Broker finds and arranges loans from lenders. Brokers are paid by a fee from you, a commissionCommissionA payment to a seller or adviser that depends on what the customer buys, which can create a conflict of interest.Read more about Commission from the lender, or both. A commission can make a broker’s income depend on which loan you choose. Asking “How are you paid for this loan, and by whom?” is a normal question.

Negotiation

Rates and fees are often not fixed. Lenders compete for borrowers they see as lower risk. A written offer from another lender gives them a figure to match or beat. Fees, especially arrangement fees, are sometimes waived on request. Lenders can say no, and so can you: not taking any offer is a valid result of comparing.

Detailed version

APR from the cash flows

The APR solves for the rate that sets the present value of all payments equal to the amount actually received. For Offer B, the borrower receives ¤10,000 but pays ¤300 at once, so the net amount is ¤9,700, repaid by 36 payments of ¤306.49:

9,700 = 306.49 × (1 − (1 + i)^−36) / i
→ i ≈ 0.7133% a month
→ nominal APR ≈ 8.56%, effective ≈ 8.91%

Offer A (no fee) has a nominal rate of 6.9%, an effective rate of 7.12%. Jurisdictions define APR differently: the EU’s annual percentage rate of charge is an effective annual rate including most costs (Directive (EU) 2023/2225), while the US Truth in Lending APR is a nominal rate (periodic rate × periods per year) (Regulation Z). Cross-border comparisons need the same convention on both sides.

APR has limits. It assumes the loan runs to term, so it can mislead when early repayment is likely, and it excludes some optional costs.

Rate-for-risk tiers

Lenders sort applicants into risk tiers from credit score, income, loan-to-value and loan purpose, and price each tier separately. A small change in one input, such as a lower debt-to-income ratio or a larger deposit, can move an applicant into another tier. This is one reason quoted rates differ from advertised ones.

Cross-subsidy

Some product features are paid for by other customers. Introductory or fee-free offers can be priced so that the lender recovers the cost from borrowers who stay beyond the introductory period, pay late, or buy add-ons. This is a description of pricing, not of any lender’s intent; the terms of each product show where costs fall.

Commission disclosure

Disclosure rules for intermediaries differ. EU consumer credit rules require a credit intermediary to say whether it works exclusively with one or more lenders and to disclose any fee the consumer pays it (Directive 2008/48/EC). In UK motor finance, commission linked to the interest rate charged was banned from 2021 (FCA).

Enquiries in scoring models

Scoring models differ in how they treat several enquiries. Some count each hard enquiry. Others treat a cluster of enquiries for the same kind of loan, within a short window, as a single search, so that rate shopping is not penalised. Credit reference agencies in each country publish how their own scores handle this.

Common misconceptions

  • “The lowest rate is the cheapest loan.” Fees and term can reverse that.
  • “Checking my rate will hurt my score.” A soft-check quote does not; a formal application can.

Varies by country

Standard comparison documents differ. In the European Union, lenders give a Standard European Consumer Credit Information form before a consumer is bound. In the United Kingdom, a “representative APR” in an advert is the rate that at least 51% of applicants accepted through that advert receive. In the United States, Truth in Lending rules require a standard disclosure of the APR, finance charge and total of payments.
