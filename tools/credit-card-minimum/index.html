What this means

Paying the minimum on ¤3,000.00 at 24 % takes 15 years 1 month and costs ¤4,801.71 in interest — 1.60 × the original balance.

The minimum here is ¤90.60 in month 1, of which ¤60.00 is interest, so the balance falls by ¤30.60. As the balance shrinks the minimum shrinks with it, which is why the timeline is long.

A fixed ¤150.00 per month clears the same balance in 26 months, with ¤869.62 of interest.

Show the maths
Each month:
  interest = balance × 24 % ÷ 12
  balance  = balance + interest
  payment  = max(¤25.00, interest + 1 % × balance)
  payment  = min(payment, balance)
  if payment ≤ interest: the balance does not go down (stop)
  balance  = balance − payment
Stops when the balance is 0.005 or less, or after 1,200 months.
Month 1: interest ¤60.00, payment ¤90.60

Assumptions and limits

  • No new spending on the card, and no fees or penalty rates. The APR stays the same.
  • Interest is added once a month at the APR divided by 12, then the payment is made. A card that charges interest daily gives a slightly different total.
  • The minimum is worked out on the balance after that month’s interest. The minimum rules here are examples; the rule for a real card is in its terms and on its statement.
  • The simulation stops after 1,200 months (100 years).
