What this means

In this series, Jan is on average 0.9001× a typical month. Dividing each value by its month’s factor gives the adjusted line, which shows the trend (+0.94 % from Dec 1 to Jan 2) instead of the calendar pattern (−13.51 %). Published “seasonally adjusted” figures do the same with a more elaborate model; both depend on the pattern being stable.

Show the maths
CMA_k = (½·raw_{k−6} + raw_{k−5} + … + raw_{k+5} + ½·raw_{k+6}) ÷ 12   (not defined for the first and last 6 months)
ratio_k = raw_k ÷ CMA_k
factor_m = mean of the ratios for calendar month m, scaled so the 12 factors average 1
SA_k = raw_k ÷ factor_(month of k)
Jan 2: SA = 100.80 ÷ 0.9001 = 111.99

Assumptions and limits

  • The method is the classical ratio-to-moving-average. Statistics offices use more elaborate methods, such as X-13ARIMA-SEATS or TRAMO/SEATS, which also handle trading days, holidays and outliers.
  • The seasonal pattern is assumed to be the same every year. When the pattern changes, the factors blur the old and new patterns together.
  • A pasted series is read as consecutive months, starting in the month chosen. Every value needs to be above 0.
  • The built-in sample is constructed (a trend of 100 + k times fixed monthly factors); it is not published data.
