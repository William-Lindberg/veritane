What this means

Offer A has the lowest yearly cost: 5.1162% effective, ¤789.52 over 36 months.

Offer C costs ¤99.37 more in total. Its ¤5.00 monthly fee adds 1.11 points to the 4.50 % rate on the sticker (nominal APR 5.6131%).

Offer B costs ¤200.00 more in total. Its ¤200.00 upfront fee adds 1.35 points to the 5.00 % rate on the sticker (nominal APR 6.3539%).

The APR puts the fees and the rate on one scale; the total cost column shows the same comparison in money.

Show the maths
Payment M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), r = rate ÷ 12 (fee added → P includes the fee)
Cash flows: CF₀ = cash received; CF_k = −(M + monthly fee), k = 1 … n
Monthly IRR x: Σ CF_k ÷ (1 + x)^k = 0, found by bisection
Nominal APR = 12 × x;  Effective APR = (1 + x)^12 − 1
Total cost = everything paid − cash received
Offer A: M = ¤299.71, x = 0.4167 %/month, nominal 5.0000%, effective 5.1162%, cost ¤789.52
Offer B: M = ¤299.71, x = 0.5295 %/month, nominal 6.3539%, effective 6.5422%, cost ¤989.52
Offer C: M = ¤297.47, x = 0.4678 %/month, nominal 5.6131%, effective 5.7597%, cost ¤888.89

Assumptions and limits

  • Each loan is repaid in equal monthly payments, with interest at the nominal rate ÷ 12 each month.
  • The APR is the yearly rate that makes the cash received equal to the value of everything paid back. The nominal APR is 12 × the monthly rate; the effective APR compounds it over 12 months.
  • Legal APR definitions differ by country (for example in which fees count and how days are counted), so a lender’s published figure can differ from this one.
  • Early repayment, payment holidays, late fees and insurance are not included.
