What this means

The payment is ¤1,199.10 per month. Over the whole loan the interest comes to ¤231,676.38 — 115.8% on top of the ¤200,000.00 borrowed.

In month 1, ¤1,000.00 of the payment is interest and ¤199.10 reduces the balance. Interest is charged on the balance that is left, so early payments are mostly interest and later ones mostly principal. Money paid off early stops being charged interest for every remaining month, which is why an extra payment early in the loan saves more interest than the same payment late in the loan.

Show the maths

Monthly rate r = 6 % ÷ 12 = 0.5000 %, number of payments n = 360.

Payment   M = P·r·(1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)      (r = 0: M = P/n)
          M = ¤1,199.10
Each month k:
  interest_k  = balance_(k−1) × r
  principal_k = min(balance_(k−1), M + extra − interest_k)
  balance_k   = balance_(k−1) − principal_k
Stops when the balance is 0.005 or less; the last payment is what is owed.
Total interest   = Σ interest_k = ¤231,676.38

Assumptions and limits

  • The interest rate is fixed for the whole term. The monthly rate is the yearly rate divided by 12 (a nominal rate, not an effective annual rate or APR).
  • Payments are made monthly, at the end of each month. The term in years is turned into a whole number of monthly payments.
  • Extra payments go straight to reducing the balance, and the scheduled payment stays the same, so the loan ends sooner.
  • Fees, insurance, taxes and early-repayment charges are not included. Some loan contracts limit or charge for extra payments.
