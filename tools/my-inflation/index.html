What this means

With your spending of ¤2,400.00 a month and the price changes for your own rates (the rates you typed), your personal inflation rate is 2.0%. The official all-items rate is 2.0%, the same to one decimal place.

The largest part of your rate comes from housing: 38% of your spending with a price change of 2.0% adds 0.75 percentage points.

The official rate weights each category by what all households in the country spend on it together. Your rate uses your own shares instead, which is why the two differ. If you bought the same things 12 months ago, they would have cost about ¤564.71 less over a year.

Show the maths
Share of spending   wₖ = sₖ / Σ s
Your rate           π = Σ wₖ × rₖ
                    = 0.375 × 2.0 + 0.188 × 2.0 + 0.104 × 2.0 + 0.063 × 2.0 + 0.021 × 2.0 + 0.063 × 2.0 + 0.188 × 2.0
                    = 2.0%
Difference          π − headline = 0.00 pp
Extra cost a year   12 × Σ (sₖ − sₖ / (1 + rₖ/100)) = ¤564.71

sₖ is your monthly spending on category k and rₖ its 12-month price change in %. This is the same idea statistics offices use for the headline rate, with household-wide shares in place of yours.

Assumptions and limits

  • Your rate is a weighted average: each category’s 12-month price change counts in proportion to your share of spending on it.
  • Each category uses one official index for the whole country or area. Prices in your town, and of the brands you buy, change by different amounts.
  • Housing uses the index for rents and housing services. Mortgage payments and house prices are not part of these indices.
  • “Everything else” uses the all-items rate, because the remaining categories are not split out here.
  • The tool uses the latest month for which every category is published, and your spending as it is today. Spending this year and last year is assumed to be the same mix.
  • Your amounts are not put in the page address. They are stored on this device when you turn on “Remember my inputs”, and not otherwise.
