What this means

Avalanche sends the money left after the minimums to the highest rate first, so the balance charged the highest rate shrinks soonest. With these debts avalanche costs ¤175.89 less in interest than snowball.

Snowball clears its first debt (Store) in month 6; avalanche clears its first debt (Card) in month 17.

A budget equal to the minimums (¤350.00) takes 59 months with avalanche and costs ¤5,864.04 in interest. The extra ¤250.00 per month brings that to 28 months and ¤1,931.26. Here the extra money changes the end date by 31 months, and the choice of method changes it by 0.

Show the maths
Every month, for both methods:
1. Interest: for each debt with a balance, balance += balance × APR / 12
2. Minimums: pay each debt min(minimum, balance); left = budget − Σ paid
3. Order the open debts:
     snowball  = smallest balance first
     avalanche = highest APR first (equal APR → smaller balance first)
4. Pay 'left' into the first debt (up to its balance), then the next
5. Repeat until every balance is 0.005 or less (cap: 1,200 months)

Avalanche: 28 months (2 years 4 months), interest ¤1,931.26

Assumptions and limits

  • Each rate stays the same until the debt is paid off. Interest is added monthly at the yearly rate divided by 12, before that month’s payment.
  • The monthly budget is the same every month. Each debt receives its minimum payment first; what is left goes to one debt at a time in the order of the chosen method.
  • When a debt is paid off, its minimum payment stays in the budget and goes to the next debt.
  • Snowball orders by the smallest balance; avalanche orders by the highest rate, and by the smaller balance when two rates are equal.
  • Fees, new spending, penalty rates and changes to minimum payments are not included. The simulation stops after 1,200 months (100 years).
