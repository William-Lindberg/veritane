What this means

Going from 3 % to 5 % changes the payment by +¤331.14 a month (+¤3,973.63 a year), which is +23.3%. Over the remaining 25 years that is +¤99,340.85 in interest. On a fixed-rate loan the new rate applies from the renewal date, not before; the +2 and +3 point rows are the kind of stress test lenders in many countries apply when they assess an application (the Affordability mode runs it on your numbers).

Show the maths
r = yearly rate ÷ 12, n = years × 12 = 300
M = P·r·(1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1)        (r = 0: M = P ÷ n)
M at 3 % = ¤1,422.63     M at 5 % = ¤1,753.77
Monthly change  = M_new − M_old        = +¤331.14
Yearly change   = 12 × monthly change  = +¤3,973.63
Total interest  = M × n − P            : ¤126,790.18 → ¤226,131.04
Interest change = +¤99,340.85

Assumptions and limits

  • The loan is a repayment loan with equal monthly payments over the remaining term. Monthly rate = yearly rate ÷ 12, as lenders use for amortisation.
  • The new rate applies from now for the whole remaining term. On a fixed-rate loan it applies from the renewal date.
  • Fees, insurance, taxes and any change in the remaining term are not included.
  • The payment-to-income limit and the stress steps are the numbers you enter. Lenders in each country set their own rules.
