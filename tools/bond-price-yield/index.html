What this means

A bond paying 5 % when the market yield is 5.00 % is priced at ¤1,000.00, at face value (at par), so that a buyer at that price earns the market yield.

Modified duration of 7.79 means: yields up 1 point → price down about 7.79 %. For this bond the duration estimate is −¤77.95 and the exact change is −¤74.39; the gap is convexity. Longer maturities and lower coupons have higher duration, so their prices move more for the same change in yield.

Show the maths
c = face × coupon / freq = ¤1,000.00 × 5 % / 2 = ¤25.00
i = yield / freq = 5.0000 % / 2 = 2.50000 %
n = years × freq = 20
Price = c × (1 − (1 + i)^−n) / i + face / (1 + i)^n = ¤1,000.00
        (if i = 0: Price = c × n + face)
Current yield = face × coupon / Price = 5.000%
Macaulay D = [Σ (t/freq) × c/(1 + i)^t + years × face/(1 + i)^n] / Price = 7.9894
Modified D = D / (1 + i) = 7.7946
ΔPrice ≈ −Dmod × Δy × Price = −7.7946 × 0.01 × 1,000.00 = -77.95

Assumptions and limits

  • The bond pays a fixed coupon on schedule and repays its face value at maturity. Default risk, call features and inflation-linked payments are not modelled.
  • The price is worked out on a coupon date, so there is no accrued interest. Quoted “clean” and “dirty” prices differ by accrued interest between coupon dates.
  • One yield discounts every cash flow, compounded at the coupon frequency. Markets price each maturity at its own rate (the yield curve).
  • The duration estimate is a straight-line approximation. For larger yield changes the exact price change differs from it; the gap is called convexity.
  • Taxes and dealing costs are not included.
