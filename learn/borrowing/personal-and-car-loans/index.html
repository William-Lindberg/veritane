Personal and Car Loans

Unsecured personal loans, vehicle finance, leases and dealer financing compared.

Intermediate·6 min (Simple) · 10 min (Detailed)·Updated 6 November 2026

Read first: Anatomy of a Loan

Key points

  • Car finance comes as a loan, hire purchase, a lease or a balloon plan, and each ends with a different answer to who owns the car.
  • Dealer finance and bank finance for the same car can differ in APR and total cost.
  • Depreciation, the fall in a car's value, is often a larger cost than the interest.
  • Add-ons such as insurance, warranties and service plans are priced separately and add to the amount borrowed when financed.
  • Negative equity happens when the amount owed is more than the car is worth.

Simple: plain words. Detailed: exact figures, codes and methods.

In short: a personal loan gives you cash to spend on anything; car finance is tied to one car. The real cost of a car loan is the interest plus the fall in the car’s value, plus anything added to the deal.

Simple version

Personal loans

A personal loan is a fixed amount, paid back in equal monthly payments over a set term, often one to seven years. Most personal loans are unsecuredUnsecured loanA loan backed only by the borrower's promise to repay.Read more about Unsecured loan: no asset backs them. The rate depends on your credit history, your income and the amount.

People use personal loans for repairs, furniture, weddings, moving costs or to combine other debts into one payment. The lender does not usually control how the money is spent.

The biggest cost is often the car itself

A new car loses value fast in its first few years. This loss is called depreciationDepreciationThe loss of value of an asset over time, or a fall in a currency's exchange rate.Read more about Depreciation.

Example

You borrow ¤20,000 (example) at 7% a year over five years to buy a car. The payment is about ¤396.02 a month. In the first three years you pay about ¤3,102 in interest.

If the car loses 40% of its value in those three years (example), that is ¤8,000. The depreciation is more than twice the interest.

So the total cost of a car is depreciation, plus interest, plus insurance, fuel or charging, tax and repairs.

Where costs hide

The price of the car is one number. Others arrive at the desk:

  • extended warranties,
  • paint or interior protection,
  • service plans,
  • payment protection or GAP insurance (it pays the difference between what the insurer pays and what you owe if the car is written off).

Each has a price. If it is added to the loan, you also pay interest on it. These products are optional in many countries; the contract shows which items are part of the finance.

Negative equity

Negative equityNegative equityOwing more on a loan than the asset it is secured on is worth.Read more about Negative equity means you owe more than the car is worth. In the example above, after one year you still owe about ¤16,538. If the car is then worth ¤15,000, the gap is ¤1,538. Selling the car would not clear the loan. Negative equity is common in the first years of a long loan with a small deposit.

Can I afford it?

Affordability is a cash-flow question. The monthly payment has to fit next to rent, food and other bills, and the car brings running costs on top. A lower payment over a longer term fits more easily each month but costs more interest in total.

Detailed version

Personal loan underwriting

Lenders assess two things: the probability of default (from credit history, existing debts and the stability of income) and affordability (whether the payment fits the borrower’s income after essential spending). Common inputs are a credit report, declared income (often verified by pay slips or bank data), the debt-to-income ratio and the loan purpose. Many lenders use risk-based pricing: applicants judged riskier are offered higher rates, which is why advertised rates are not offered to everyone.

Dealer finance

Car dealers often act as credit brokers: they arrange finance from a lender and can receive a commission. Two consequences follow. First, the APR offered at a dealer can differ from a bank’s quote for the same borrower. Second, a “0% finance” offer is often paid for by the manufacturer or reflected in a higher cash price or a smaller discount; the total paid under each option shows the difference.

In the United Kingdom, the regulator banned discretionary commission arrangements in motor finance, under which a broker’s commission rose with the interest rate it set, from January 2021, and has run work on complaints about past arrangements (Financial Conduct Authority). In the European Union, consumer credit rules require credit intermediaries to state whether they work with one lender or several and any fee the consumer pays them (Directive 2008/48/EC). In the United States, Truth in Lending disclosures show the APR and finance charge whether the credit is arranged by a dealer or a bank (Regulation Z).

Residual value and lease pricing

A lease charges for the expected depreciation plus a finance charge. With a capitalised cost C, a residual value R at the end of the term and n months:

depreciation part = (C − R) / n
finance part      = (C + R) × money factor
money factor      ≈ APR / 2400

The residual value is the lessor’s forecast. A high residual makes payments lower; if the car is worth less than R at the end, the lessor carries the loss in a closed-end lease.

GAP insurance economics

If a financed car is written off, a standard motor policy pays roughly its market value. GAP cover pays some or all of the difference between that payout and the outstanding finance (or the original price). Its value to the buyer depends on how large the gap is likely to be (larger with small deposits, long terms and fast-depreciating models) and on the price, which varies widely between sellers. The expected-value approach in the Insurance module applies.

Negative equity arithmetic

Equity = market value − outstanding balance. Amortisation reduces the balance slowly at first while depreciation is fastest early, so equity is often negative in the first years. Rolling negative equity into the next car loan raises the new principal and lengthens the period of negative equity.

Common misconceptions

  • “The monthly payment shows the cost.” The total paid, the APR and the depreciation show it.
  • “A 0% deal is free credit.” The cost can sit in the price; a cash-price comparison reveals it.
  • “Hire purchase and a lease are the same.” In hire purchase the car can become yours; in a lease it does not.

Varies by country

Protection for car finance borrowers differs. In the United Kingdom, a hire purchase customer has a legal right to end the agreement early once half of the total price has been paid, and after a third has been paid the lender needs a court order to repossess the car. In the European Union, consumer credit rules give a right to repay early, with compensation to the lender capped by law. In the United States, repossession rules are set by each state, and in many states a lender can repossess a car after a default without going to court.
