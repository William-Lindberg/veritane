Net Worth and Tracking Progress

The one number that summarises where you stand, and how to watch it change over time.

Everyday·5 min (Simple) · 8 min (Detailed)·Updated 6 November 2026

Read first: Income, Expenses and Cash Flow

Key points

  • Net worth is what you own minus what you owe.
  • It can be negative and still be improving; early in adult life it is often negative, for example while a student loan is being repaid.
  • Checking it every few months shows the trend; day-to-day changes are mostly noise.
  • A complete picture includes pensions and debts, not only cash.
  • The direction of travel tells you more than the level on any single date.

Simple version

One subtraction

net worth = what you own − what you owe

What you own are your assetsAssetAnything owned that has value, such as cash, property, investments or a car.Read more about Asset: cash, savings, pension savings, investments, a home, a car. What you owe are your liabilitiesLiabilityMoney owed to someone else.Read more about Liability: loans, credit card balances, a mortgage, money owed to family.

A one-page net worth sheet

Example

A young worker lists everything (example):

Assets Liabilities
Current account ¤2,000 Student loan ¤15,000
Savings ¤3,000 Credit card ¤1,000
Pension savings ¤8,000 Car loan ¤4,000
Car (resale value) ¤6,000
Total ¤19,000 Total ¤20,000

Net worth: ¤19,000 − ¤20,000 = −¤1,000.

A year later: assets ¤22,500 (cash ¤2,500, savings ¤4,000, pension ¤10,500, car ¤5,500) and liabilities ¤17,000 (student loan ¤14,000, car loan ¤3,000, card paid off). Net worth is now ¤22,500 − ¤17,000 = ¤5,500, a change of +¤6,500 in a year.

What to include

  • Assets: bank balances, savings, pension pots (the current value, if known), investments, a home at a realistic sale price, a car at its resale value.
  • Liabilities: every debt at its current balance, including student loans, overdrafts, buy-now-pay-later plans and informal loans.

Everyday belongings such as furniture and clothes are usually left out, because they are hard to value and rarely sold.

Why negative is normal early on

Many people start their working life owing more than they own. A student loan or a first car loan comes before years of earnings and savings. A negative number is a starting point, not a verdict. What matters is whether it is moving up.

How often to look

Prices of homes and investments move every day, so a daily net worth would jump up and down without telling you much. Many people update their sheet every three months, or once a year at the same time. A yearly review can also be a moment to check goals, insurance and the budget.

Where the change comes from

Net worth rises in three ways:

  1. Saving: a cash-flow surplus adds to assets.
  2. Paying down debt: each repayment of principal lowers liabilities.
  3. Changes in value: investments, pensions or a home rise or fall in price.

The first two are within your plan; the third is not. Separating them shows how much of a change came from your own decisions.

Your sheet stays with you

Net worth figures are personal financial information. Veritane’s net worth tracker works only on your device: the sheet stays on it, there is no account, and there is no share link. Storing the sheet on the device is optional, and you can export it to a file.

Detailed version

The household balance sheet

A household balance sheetBalance sheetA snapshot of what is owned and what is owed at one point in time.Read more about Balance sheet lists assets and liabilities at a point in time. Net worth is the balancing item:

assets = liabilities + net worth
change in net worth = saving + net revaluations (+ net transfers received, such as gifts or inheritances)

The cash-flow statement (lesson 1.2) explains the saving part; revaluations explain the rest. Separating the two in a tracker avoids reading a rise in house prices as extra saving.

Valuing illiquid assets

  • Home: a recent sale price of similar homes, less the cost of selling, is a more cautious figure than the purchase price or an asking price.
  • Car: resale value, which falls with age and distance.
  • Pensions: for a defined contribution pension, the current pot value. A defined benefit pension (a promised income) has no pot; its value can be estimated as the present value of the expected payments, which depends heavily on the discount rate.
  • Small business or partnership stakes: hard to value; a conservative estimate or a separate line is common.

Consistency over time matters more than precision: a tracker that values the home the same way each quarter shows a meaningful trend.

Human capital

Human capital is the present value of a person’s future earnings. For a young worker with decades of earnings ahead, it can be larger than all other assets combined, but it is not tradable and appears on no balance sheet. It explains why a negative net worth at 25 can coexist with high expected future earnings, and why insurance against loss of income is part of protecting a balance sheet.

Solvency and liquidity

  • Solvency: assets exceed liabilities (net worth above zero).
  • Liquidity: enough assets can be turned into cash quickly to meet payments due.

A household can be solvent but illiquid (most of its wealth in a home or pension, with bills due now) or liquid but insolvent (cash on hand, but debts larger than all assets). An emergency fund addresses liquidity; net worth measures solvency.

Distribution of household wealth

Central banks run surveys of household wealth, such as the Federal Reserve’s Survey of Consumer Finances in the United States (Federal Reserve) and the Eurosystem’s Household Finance and Consumption Survey in the euro area (ECB). These surveys report wealth by age, income and household type, using medians as well as means. Wealth is typically distributed much more unequally than income, and median net worth rises with age through most of working life. Survey design, the treatment of pensions and the inclusion of very wealthy households differ, so figures from different countries are compared with care.

Common misconceptions

  • “A high income means a high net worth.” Net worth depends on what is saved and kept, not on what is earned.
  • “Paying off debt does not count as progress because savings did not grow.” Reducing a liability raises net worth by the same amount.

Varies by country

How debts behave affects how people read them on a net worth sheet. In the United Kingdom and Australia, many student loans are repaid as a share of income above a threshold, and unpaid balances can be written off after a set period or in certain circumstances. In the United States, many student loans have fixed monthly payments regardless of income, although income-driven repayment plans exist. In some countries, higher education is largely tax-funded and student debt is rare.

Why people disagree

What belongs in a personal net worth figure is debated. Some include their main home at market value, arguing that it is the largest asset many households own. Others leave it out or show it separately, because a home that is lived in cannot easily be spent and selling it means paying for somewhere else to live. Similar debates apply to pensions that cannot be accessed until retirement, and to the value of future state pension rights, which most surveys and trackers do not include.
