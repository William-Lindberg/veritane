IMF forecasts and what happened

The IMF publishes projections for inflation and economic growth in every country. Veritane keeps a copy each time the values change, so they can be compared with what the IMF later estimates actually happened.

Projections compared with outcomes

Each row compares a projection made before the year began with the IMF’s latest estimate for that year, published after the year ended.

The comparison starts with Veritane’s first snapshot on 7 November 2026. Older IMF projections are not filled in from other sources: the comparison uses the values Veritane copied itself and no others.

Veritane started keeping copies of IMF projections on 7 November 2026. The first comparison of a projection with an outcome becomes possible once the IMF publishes estimates for 2027, which it normally does from early 2028.

How to read this

  • A projection is the IMF’s view of a year that has not ended. An estimate is its view of a year that has ended; it can still change as countries revise their data.
  • The IMF’s data service does not say which edition its values come from, so Veritane labels each copy by the date it first saw the values.
  • A difference is the latest estimate minus the projection, in percentage points. It says how far the projection was from the later estimate, not why.

Source: IMF, World Economic Outlook (DataMapper) (opens in a new tab) · 7 Nov 2026
