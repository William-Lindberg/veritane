Sales Tax, VAT, Excise and Property Taxes

The taxes paid when spending and owning: how VAT and sales tax are collected, what excise duties target, and how property taxes work.

Intermediate·5 min (Simple) · 9 min (Professional)·Updated 6 November 2026

Key points

  • VAT (called GST in some countries) is collected at each stage of production, with each business deducting the tax it paid on its inputs; a sales tax is charged once, at the final sale.
  • Excise duties are taxes on specific goods such as fuel, alcohol, tobacco and, in some countries, sugary drinks.
  • Property taxes are charged on owning land and buildings and pay for local services in many countries.
  • Taxes on spending take a larger share of income from households that spend most of what they earn; exemptions, reduced rates and rebates are common responses.
  • Whether a displayed price includes tax varies by country.
Depth:

In short: some taxes are charged on what people earn; others are charged on what they buy and own. This lesson covers the second group.

Simple version

A receipt, taken apart

Many receipts show a line for tax. Suppose a pair of shoes costs ¤100 before tax, and the tax rate is 20% (example).

  • Price before tax: ¤100
  • Tax at 20%: ¤20
  • Price you pay: ¤120

A common mistake is to work backwards from ¤120 by taking 20% of it. That gives ¤24, which is too much. The tax inside a tax-inclusive price is price × rate ÷ (100% + rate): ¤120 × 20 ÷ 120 = ¤20.

VAT and sales tax

Two designs are common.

Value-added taxValue-added tax (VAT / GST)A tax added at each stage of production and collected in the final price.Read more about Value-added tax (VAT / GST) (VAT), called GST (goods and services tax) in some countries, is charged at every stage. Each business adds tax to its sales and subtracts the tax it already paid on what it bought. So each business pays tax only on the value it added.

Example

A 20% VAT (example), three businesses:

Stage Sale price before VAT VAT charged VAT already paid on inputs VAT sent to the tax office
Grower sells flour to a baker ¤40 ¤8 ¤0 ¤8
Baker sells bread to a shop ¤70 ¤14 ¤8 ¤6
Shop sells to customers ¤100 ¤20 ¤14 ¤6

The tax office receives 8 + 6 + 6 = ¤20, which is 20% of the final price of ¤100. The customer pays it all in the price.

A sales tax is charged only once, when the final customer buys. Businesses buying for resale do not pay it.

Excise duties

An excise dutyExcise taxA tax on specific goods such as fuel, alcohol or tobacco.Read more about Excise tax is a tax on one kind of product. Fuel, alcohol and tobacco are taxed this way in many countries, and some countries also tax sugary drinks. Excise is often charged per unit, such as per litre, rather than as a percentage of the price. These taxes raise money and also make the product more expensive, which tends to reduce how much is bought.

Property taxes

Many places charge a yearly tax on owning a home or land. It is usually based on the property’s value, or on a band of values, and it often pays for local services such as rubbish collection, schools or roads. In some countries tenants pay a local tax too, and in others it falls only on owners.

Who carries the load?

A household with a low income often spends all of it. A household with a high income often saves part of it. A tax on spending therefore takes a larger share of a low income.

Example

Two households, 20% tax on spending (example). Household A earns ¤20,000 and spends ¤15,000 on taxed goods: tax ¤3,000, or 15% of income. Household B earns ¤100,000 and spends ¤50,000 on taxed goods: tax ¤10,000, or 10% of income.

Many countries respond with lower rates or no tax on basics such as food, or with cash rebates for low-income households.

Prices when travelling

In some countries the shelf price includes all taxes. In others, tax is added at the till. Professional version

VAT mechanics

VAT payable for a period = output VAT (on sales) − input VAT (on purchases)

A negative result is a refund. The credit mechanism means VAT does not cascade: the tax on a final good equals the rate times its final price, however many stages it passes through. A turnover tax without credits does cascade, which favours firms that merge stages. Exemptions break the chain: an exempt business charges no VAT but cannot reclaim input VAT, so tax is embedded in its costs. Zero-rating charges VAT at 0% and allows full input credit.

Destination and origin

EU VAT on cross-border trade in goods follows, in general, the destination principle: exports leave free of VAT and imports are taxed where they are consumed, so goods compete under the importing country’s rate (European Commission). Under an origin principle, tax would be charged where goods are produced.

Excise and Pigouvian taxes

Excise duties in the EU apply to energy products, alcohol and manufactured tobacco under common rules with national rates (European Commission). A Pigouvian tax, named after the economist Arthur Pigou, sets the tax per unit equal to the marginal external cost: the harm one more unit does to people outside the transaction. Carbon prices are an example of this logic. In practice, external costs are estimated with wide ranges, and excise rates also reflect revenue needs.

Property tax assessment

property tax = assessed value × rate        (value-based systems)
property tax = amount for the band          (banded systems)

Assessed values are set by revaluation, which happens at intervals; between revaluations, assessed and market values drift apart. England’s Council Tax uses bands of property value and is charged to the occupier, owner or tenant (GOV.UK).

Customs duties

A tariffTariffA tax on imported goods, collected at the border and often passed on to buyers.Read more about Tariff is a tax on imports, charged at the border. Unlike VAT on imports, which is matched by VAT on domestic goods, a tariff applies only to the foreign good, so it changes relative prices between home and foreign producers. Its effects on trade are covered in the Macroeconomics module.

Measuring regressivity

Consumption taxes look regressive when measured against current income, because low-income households spend a larger share of their income. Measured against current spending, a single-rate VAT is roughly proportional. Measured against lifetime income, the picture lies in between, because people save in some years and spend savings in others. Analysts state which measure they use.

Common misconceptions

  • “Businesses pay VAT.” Businesses collect and remit it; the economic incidence falls mainly on final consumption, though in competitive markets part can be absorbed in margins.
  • “Reduced rates on food only help low-income households.” Higher-income households also buy food, often more in ¤ terms, so they receive a larger ¤ benefit from the same reduced rate.

Varies by country

Price display and design differ. In the EU, consumers must be told the total price including all taxes (Your Europe). In Canada, GST or HST is charged on most sales and is often added at the till rather than included in the shelf price (Canada Revenue Agency). The United States has no national VAT; sales taxes are set by states and local governments and are usually added at the till.
