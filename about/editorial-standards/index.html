Editorial standards

These rules apply to every news item, figure, tool, scam guide, explainer and glossary entry on Veritane. They are public so you can hold us to them.

1. Primary sources only (news)

News items are produced only from documents published by: central banks; national and international statistics offices (for example Eurostat, ONS, BLS, SCB, Statistics Canada, ABS, IBGE); the IMF, World Bank, BIS, OECD and UN agencies; national treasuries and finance ministries; financial regulators and consumer-protection agencies. We do not use news agencies, newspapers, analyst notes, company press releases, social media or anonymous sources as the basis for a news item. The full list of sources, with the exact feed or data service we read, is on the Sources page.

2. Link every claim

Every number, quote and date links to the exact source document, series or table, with the publication date and the date we fetched it. If we cannot link it, we do not publish it.

How we check numbers

This section states what “checked” means on Veritane, so the word is not a promise without a method.

  • Software reads each figure from the publisher’s own dataset or data service, the one named and linked on the item, and stores the time it read it (“retrieved”).
  • Before a data file is published, the software checks every figure: it has to be a number, with a period, a unit, a source name and an https link to the source. A figure that fails these checks is not published.
  • For 319 of 1091 measured series, the value is also stored as the source wrote it, next to the rounded figure shown on the page.
  • Where Veritane calculates a figure from the source’s own index (for example a 12-month change), the software recalculates it from the stored inputs and stops publication when the result does not match.
  • Figure pages have a “Check this yourself” panel with the dataset code and a link to the source (1091 of 1091 measured series). For 913 of them the link opens the same observation; for the others it opens the dataset or the request Veritane made.
  • When a publisher changes a figure it published earlier, the change is listed on the revisions page. Revisions log
  • No person reads a figure before it is published. Volunteers review items after publication, and each item shows its review status.

The list of words the software checks for in generated text is public. Words the software checks for

3. Facts and analysis are separated and labelled

A news item has at most three labelled parts:

  • What was published: facts. Who, what, when, the numbers, the official wording.
  • Context: facts only. The previous value, the same period last year, the long-run average, the official target if one exists.
  • Analysis: optional, always headed “Analysis”, always attributed to a named volunteer, never generated by software. This part is switched off at launch. It stays off until at least two named reviewers are listed on the Contribute page, and switching it on is announced on this page.

Explainers use a “Key points” box for the facts and a separate “Why people disagree” section where interpretations differ.

4. Neutral wording

We describe; we do not judge or forecast. We use the words in the left column and leave out the words in the right column.

Other rules: no exclamation marks; no rhetorical questions in news; no adjectives of size in place of a number (give the number); no first-person opinion outside “Analysis”; no emojis; no country stereotypes.

4a. One list of loaded words, checked by software

A single list of loaded words (verbs of speed or force, hedges, speculation, causal shortcuts and judgement words) applies to the whole site. Software checks it automatically on every build: strictly on generated news text, and on hand-written lessons, glossary entries, Protect pages and tool explanations. Nothing on the site attributes a motive to a lender, seller, employer or government unless it quotes a regulator’s finding, with a citation. Claims about what happened in the past appear only with a dataset, a date range and a link. Figures of speech are not used in titles; a title names the mechanism.

4b. Safety facts (the one exception)

Veritane does not tell readers what to do with their money. The one exception: a Protect page may contain a box headed “Safety facts” that uses direct instructions (for example about never sharing a one-time passcode), only when every line (a) is about preventing fraud or crime, not a financial choice, and (b) is attributed to a named regulator, police service or consumer authority, with a link. Lessons and tools never use this exception.

5. Both sides of contested questions

Where economists or the public disagree (for example minimum-wage effects, rent control, public spending cuts, crypto-assets, tax levels, central-bank independence, trade policy), a lesson: (a) states the question neutrally; (b) presents the strongest version of at least two positions with the evidence each side cites, linked; (c) says what the evidence does and does not settle; (d) does not conclude for the reader. If a volunteer cannot write the opposing view fairly, the lesson is not published until someone can.

6. What we do not publish

  • Investment recommendations, price targets, “buy / sell / hold”, or predictions of asset prices, rates or elections.
  • Content about individual listed companies, except where a regulator, court or statistics office publishes about them.
  • Political endorsements or party comparisons.
  • Anything we cannot source.

7. Corrections

See the Corrections page. In short: we aim to check a report within 72 hours, the fix is noted on the page itself, and the correction is logged publicly.

8. No advertising, no affiliate links, no sponsored content

Veritane carries no advertising of any kind, no affiliate or referral links, no sponsored or “partner” content and no product placements. Links to commercial sites appear only when they are the primary source (for example a regulator’s notice that names a company). We do not accept free products, services or paid trips. Volunteers disclose any employment in finance, politics or public communications, and the disclosure is listed with their name on the Contribute page.

9. Automated summaries

News summaries on this site are generated by software from official data releases and documents, using fixed sentence templates (not a free-writing AI model). They are published automatically, without a person reading them first. The software can only fill a fixed template with the number the source published, and every item links to the source, so you can check it in one click. Volunteers check items after publication when they can. Each item shows the template version, the source, the fetch time and its review status: Not yet reviewed (the default for every new item) or Reviewed with the volunteer’s name and the date (a volunteer compared it with the source). We do not claim that items are checked before you see them. If the software and the source disagree, the source is correct; please report the difference.

10. Use of AI tools

Volunteers use AI assistants to help draft, translate, check text and write software. The volunteer who submits the text is responsible for its accuracy and for checking every source link by hand. AI-drafted text is not published without a human reviewer, and AI is not used to write “Analysis”.

11. Accessibility and language

Every page aims to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA (the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines). Every explainer has a Simple version. Translations are reviewed by a second speaker of the language before publication. Accessibility problems can be reported like any other error.

12. Independence

No person or organisation can pay for, or otherwise obtain, changes to content. Content decisions are made against these standards. The founder has no veto over corrections.

13. Outlet reports and how we find them

Veritane writes its own stories. Alongside them, News shows “Other reports”: headlines from listed news outlets, quoted as published, attributed and linked, with the time we saw them. They are kept apart from official figures, and their text is not shown or stored. Reports are found with the GDELT Project. Outlet policy

Importance

Other reports carry an importance score from 0 to 100, counted from coverage and official links with published weights. It does not measure whether a report is true or how much it matters to you. How ranking works

Linked and not linked

A report is “Linked to an official release” when an official release we publish matches its country, dates and subject. This claims that the release exists and links it; it does not claim that each sentence in the reports is correct. “Not linked to an official release yet” claims that none of the official sources we follow has a matching release yet, and nothing more. Veritane does not describe outlet reports as “confirmed” or “verified”.

Forecasts

Forecasts, such as IMF projections, are shown as forecasts with their date and source, not as published figures. A headline that describes a forecast or a view is marked as such.
